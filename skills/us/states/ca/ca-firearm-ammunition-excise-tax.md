# California Firearm and Ammunition Excise Tax (CFET), 11% on retail sales v0.1

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from the California Firearm Excise Tax Law (Revenue and Taxation Code 36001 to 36043) and Fee Collection Procedures Law sections 55042, 55044 and 55050 as published in the CDTFA Business Taxes Law Guide (Revision 2026); the codified section history in the official California Legislature bulk data archive; CDTFA's Tax Guide for Sellers of Firearm and Ammunition Products (Getting Started, Industry Topics, FAQs and Resources pages); CDTFA's CFET return filing instructions; CDTFA's special taxes and fees rate table; CDTFA special notices L-923, L-947, L-953, L-955, L-958 and L-996; and form CDTFA-230-FET. All were read on 2026-11-23 (California, Pacific time). No accountant has reviewed it.

**Answer first.** "Commencing July 1, 2024, an excise tax is hereby imposed upon licensed firearms dealers, firearms manufacturers, and ammunition vendors, at the rate of 11 percent of the gross receipts from the retail sale in this state of any firearm, firearm precursor part, or ammunition" (R&TC 36011). The seller owes it, on top of sales tax, whether or not it passes the cost on: "a retailer may optionally collect CFET reimbursement from their customers" ([CDTFA Industry Topics](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/sellers-of-firearm-and-ammunition-products/industry-topics.htm)). Each seller registers for a CFET certificate of registration and files a **quarterly** return electronically, due with payment "on or before the last day of the month next succeeding each quarterly period" (R&TC 36032, 36033, 36035). A return is due every quarter, even with no sales or only exempt sales.

**Two exemptions** (R&TC 36021): retail sales "to any active or retired peace officer or any law enforcement agency employing that peace officer"; and all retail sales by a seller in any quarter in which its total gross receipts from retail sales of firearms, precursor parts and ammunition are "less than five thousand dollars ($5,000)". The second is a cliff: USD 4,999 in a quarter is exempt in full, USD 5,000 is taxed in full.

**What is in the base** ([CDTFA Industry Topics](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/sellers-of-firearm-and-ammunition-products/industry-topics.htm); [CDTFA L-955](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/L955.pdf)): the sales price plus separately stated charges such as merchant credit card fees, handling and transfer fees and fabrication labor. Out: the state fees the purchaser pays (Dealer Record of Sale, Firearm Safety Act, Safety and Enforcement fees), sales tax, and CFET reimbursement. CDTFA's own example: USD 500 firearm plus a USD 10 card fee gives "CFET due (11% x $510) $ 56.10" ([CDTFA L-947](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/l947.pdf)).

CDTFA: "If the due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or state holiday, returns must be received by the next business day to be on time" ([CDTFA Getting Started](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/sellers-of-firearm-and-ammunition-products/getting-started.htm)). CDTFA's payment paragraph on the same page does not repeat that sentence for payments; pay by the statutory date, or confirm with CDTFA.

**Tax year.** Tax year label 2026-27: this skill covers the quarters of 2026 and the first half of 2027, with returns due into 2027. The rate is 11% from 1 July 2024 ([CDTFA special taxes and fees rates](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/special-taxes-and-fees-tax-rates/)). The archive's section history shows no amendment to R&TC 36011, 36021 or 36031 to 36043 since AB 28, and none of the 96 tax, fee, budget and firearm bills chaptered in 2026 in the same archive amends R&TC 36001 to 36043; no 2026 change to the tax, including one operative in 2027, was found as of the read date.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope: a "licensed firearms dealer" (Penal Code 26700), a "firearms manufacturer" (federally licensed and making retail sales to consumers in California) and an "ammunition vendor" (Penal Code) (R&TC 36001(a), (d), (g)), and their bookkeepers and advisers. The job: decide what is taxable in a quarter, compute the CFET, and register, file and pay on time.

Out of scope, and where it goes:

| Topic | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Whether an item is a "firearm", "firearm precursor part" or "ammunition" under the Penal Code definitions R&TC 36001 adopts | DOJ Bureau of Firearms (CDTFA's FAQ sends item questions there); not covered here |
| Sales and use tax rates, returns and exemptions, beyond how they interact with CFET | california-sales-tax; CDTFA |
| Federal firearms and ammunition excise tax (IRC 4181) | refer; not covered here |
| Local firearm or ammunition taxes and fees (R&TC 36042 preserves them) | refer to the city or county |
| DOJ, ATF and Federal Firearms License requirements | DOJ and ATF |
| Validity challenges to the tax | refer |

## Ask the client first

- Is the business a licensed California firearms dealer, a federally licensed firearms manufacturer selling to California consumers, an ammunition vendor, or an out-of-state seller? Does it hold a seller's permit and a CFET certificate of registration?
- What were the quarter's retail sales of firearms, precursor parts and ammunition, by sale price and by each separately stated charge (card, handling, transfer, fabrication, shipping)? Which state fees (DROS, Firearm Safety Act, Safety and Enforcement) were collected?
- Which sales went to active or retired peace officers or law enforcement agencies, and is there a CDTFA-230-FET certificate for each?
- When did each customer take possession? Were any paid for in one quarter and picked up in the next (waiting periods, layaway)?
- Were any firearms transferred for an out-of-state retailer or private party, or sold on consignment? Is the out-of-state retailer engaged in business in California?
- Did the business charge customers CFET reimbursement on sales that turned out to be exempt?

### Steps

1. **Is the seller liable?** The tax is imposed on licensed firearms dealers, firearms manufacturers and ammunition vendors (R&TC 36011). A "firearms manufacturer" is a federally licensed manufacturer "that engages in any retail sale of a firearm or firearm precursor part to a consumer in California" (R&TC 36001(d)). CDTFA: "Out-of-state retailers are not required to register with us for a CFET certificate of registration" ([CDTFA Industry Topics](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/sellers-of-firearm-and-ammunition-products/industry-topics.htm)); the California dealer that delivers for them owes the CFET (step 6).
2. **Register.** Register online for a CFET certificate of registration; "No fee shall be charged" (R&TC 36035). CDTFA: "a CFET account is separate from your seller's permit" ([CDTFA L-953](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/L953.pdf)). CDTFA may revoke a certificate after notice and a hearing and must tell DOJ (R&TC 36037, 36038).
3. **List the quarter's retail sales and put each in the right quarter.** A sale is a "retail sale" as in R&TC 6007 (R&TC 36001(i)(1)), so a sale for resale on a valid resale certificate is not subject to CFET; an FFL "does not qualify as a resale or exemption certificate" (CDTFA Industry Topics). A sale occurs "when there is a transfer of title or possession (whichever occurs first)"; "In the absence of an explicit title clause that passes title sooner, the sale is deemed to be made when possession of the property sold transfers to the purchaser" ([CDTFA Getting Started](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/sellers-of-firearm-and-ammunition-products/getting-started.htm)). For a firearm, neither can pass before the waiting period ends: paid on 28 June and collected on 8 July, it belongs to July's quarter ([CDTFA FAQs](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/sellers-of-firearm-and-ammunition-products/frequently-asked-questions.htm); [CDTFA L-958](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/L958.pdf)). Ammunition and parts follow the same title-or-possession rule, with no waiting period. New and used goods are both taxable. Firearm rentals are retail sales except rentals under USD 20 a day used only on the premises; a gun club that supplies ammunition for a single lump-sum price is the consumer of it, and "the vendor's retail sale of ammunition to you is subject to the CFET" (CDTFA Industry Topics).
4. **Measure gross receipts.** "Gross receipts" has the sales tax meaning (R&TC 6012; R&TC 36001(e)). Per CDTFA (Industry Topics; FAQs; [L-955](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/L955.pdf)):
   - **In:** the sales price; separately stated "Merchant credit card fees", "Discretionary fees", handling and transfer fees, "Fabrication labor charges, such as the labor included in custom builds"; shipping to the delivering California dealer; the full lump-sum price of a required bundle (a USD 300 firearm-and-holster package is taxed at USD 300); for "buy one, get one free", the price actually charged.
   - **Out:** "Sales tax and CFET reimbursement"; the "Dealer Record of Sale (DROS) fee", "Firearm Safety Act fee" and "Safety and Enforcement fee" paid by the purchaser.
   - CDTFA's return instructions: the CFET retail sales price "will be the same as the amount used to calculate the selling price for sales and use tax purposes" ([CDTFA CFET return](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/online-filing-instructions/CA-Firearm-and-Ammunition-Tax-Return.htm)).
5. **Apply the exemptions.**
   - Peace officers and law enforcement agencies: deduct those sales on the return. CDTFA-230-FET documents them: "For sales to peace officers, a certificate should be completed for every transaction"; an agency may give one general certificate.
   - Under USD 5,000 in the quarter: no CFET for that quarter. The return is still due.
   - Sales to the U.S. government and sales in interstate or foreign commerce are generally "not subject to the CFET" (CDTFA Industry Topics).
6. **Transfers for others and consignment** (CDTFA Industry Topics; [CDTFA L-996](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/L996.pdf)):
   - **Out-of-state retailer engaged in business in California:** from 1 October 2025 the California dealer that "transfers physical possession ... on behalf of an out-of-state retailer engaged in business in this state shall be deemed the retailer" for CFET (R&TC 36001(i)(2), added by SB 132). The dealer pays CFET; the out-of-state retailer "remains responsible for reporting and paying the California use tax" (L-996).
   - **Out-of-state retailer not engaged in business in California, or out-of-state private party:** per CDTFA, the California dealer is the retailer for both sales tax and CFET, measured on what the purchaser pays excluding state fees. Get the sales contract or invoice.
   - **Consignment:** a dealer that takes a California private party's firearm to find a buyer is the retailer and owes sales tax and CFET. Not if it only does the paperwork on a sale the parties agreed in advance and never takes title (CDTFA Industry Topics).
   - **Gifts and raffles:** a dealer buying firearm products to give away is the consumer; its supplier owes CFET on that retail sale; giving the item away is not a retail sale (CDTFA FAQs; L-955).
7. **Compute and handle reimbursement.** CFET = 11% x (gross receipts less exempt sales), or nil in a quarter under USD 5,000. If CFET reimbursement was charged on exempt sales, refund it to the customers or report it in the return's "Excess CFET reimbursement collected" field and pay it over (CDTFA CFET return; L-958). Do not include CFET reimbursement in the sales tax measure (CDTFA Industry Topics).
8. **File and pay.** File the CFET return online by the due date in the table above, even with nothing due, and pay; also file the separate sales and use tax return (L-953). EFT is required at an average of USD 20,000 a month (R&TC 55050). Keep records at least four years (CDTFA Getting Started).
9. **Late.** "[A] penalty of 10 percent of the amount of the fee, together with interest on that fee at the modified adjusted rate per month, or fraction thereof" for late payment, 10% for a late return, and the penalties "limited to a maximum of 10 percent of the fee ... for any one return" (R&TC 55042(a) to (c)). Relief for reasonable cause is available under R&TC 55044.

**AUDIT FLASH POINT** (the USD 5,000 quarterly cliff and the base). R&TC 36021(b) exempts every sale in a quarter in which "the total gross receipts from the retail sales of firearms, firearm precursor parts, or ammunition by that licensed firearms dealer, firearms manufacturer, or ammunition vendor is less than five thousand dollars ($5,000)". No source read says whether sales exempt as peace-officer sales count toward that total, or whether the test is per registrant or per business location. Small sellers near the line, sellers that split activity between entities, and sellers that drop separately stated card, transfer or fabrication charges from the base are the usual audit points. CDTFA's notices say that if you collected CFET reimbursement in an exempt quarter you must refund it or pay it over.

## Worked cases

Both cases are made up for illustration.

**Case 1: a dealer's October to December 2026 return.**

- **Facts:** a licensed California firearms dealer, registered for CFET and a seller's permit. Retail transactions for the quarter, all collected by the customers in the quarter:
  - Firearm and ammunition sales at USD 180,000, plus separately stated merchant card fees of USD 2,400 and transfer and handling fees of USD 3,600.
  - State fees collected from purchasers (DROS, Firearm Safety Act, Safety and Enforcement): USD 4,500.
  - Sales to active and retired peace officers, with CDTFA-230-FET certificates: USD 12,000.
  - A consignment sale of a California resident's shotgun: USD 1,500.
  - Transfers for an out-of-state retailer engaged in business in California, on invoices totalling USD 6,000 (excluding state fees).
  - A rifle paid for on 25 September 2026 and collected on 5 October 2026: USD 2,000. (It is left out of the July to September return.)
  - A sale of ammunition to another dealer on a resale certificate: USD 8,000.
- **Gross receipts:** 180,000 + 2,400 + 3,600 + 12,000 + 1,500 + 6,000 + 2,000 = USD 207,500. The state fees and the resale are out.
- **Less exempt peace officer sales:** USD 12,000. **Subject to CFET:** USD 195,500.
- **CFET:** 11% x 195,500 = **USD 21,505.00**, due 31 January 2027 (a Sunday; CDTFA treats a return received on Monday 1 February 2027 as on time).
- **Sales tax measure, for contrast:** the peace officer sales stay in, but the transfers for the out-of-state retailer engaged in business in California do not (that retailer owes the use tax): 207,500 - 6,000 = USD 201,500 at the local rate (not computed here). This assumes none of the USD 3,600 transfer and handling fees relate to those out-of-state transfers.

```oa-check
name: CFET quarterly return, October to December 2026, dealer
inputs:
  sales_price: 180000
  card_fees: 2400
  transfer_handling_fees: 3600
  state_fees_collected: 4500
  peace_officer_sales: 12000
  consignment_sale: 1500
  out_of_state_engaged_transfers: 6000
  carried_in_from_prior_quarter: 2000
  resale_to_dealer: 8000
  rate: 0.11
  threshold: 5000
steps:
  - gross_receipts: sales_price + card_fees + transfer_handling_fees + peace_officer_sales + consignment_sale + out_of_state_engaged_transfers + carried_in_from_prior_quarter
  - taxable_quarter: 1 - min(1, ceil(max(0, threshold - gross_receipts)))
  - subject_to_cfet: gross_receipts - peace_officer_sales
  - cfet: round(subject_to_cfet * rate * taxable_quarter, 2)
  - sales_tax_measure: gross_receipts - out_of_state_engaged_transfers
expected:
  gross_receipts: 207500
  taxable_quarter: 1
  subject_to_cfet: 195500
  cfet: 21505.00
  sales_tax_measure: 201500
```

**Case 2: an ammunition vendor either side of USD 5,000, and reimbursement it should not have kept.**

- **Facts:** a small ammunition vendor, all sales at retail to the public, no peace officer sales (so the open question on what counts toward the USD 5,000 total does not arise). It adds an 11% CFET charge to every receipt.
  - **January to March 2027:** retail gross receipts USD 4,950; CFET reimbursement charged USD 544.50.
  - **April to June 2027:** retail gross receipts USD 5,050.
- **First quarter:** under USD 5,000, so the CFET is nil. The USD 544.50 charged to customers was not owed. The vendor decides between two routes:
  - refund it to the customers: CFET return shows nil;
  - keep it: report USD 544.50 as excess CFET reimbursement and pay it to CDTFA by 30 April 2027.
  Either way the vendor keeps none of it.
- **Second quarter:** USD 5,050 is not under USD 5,000, so all of it is taxed: 11% x 5,050 = **USD 555.50**, due 31 July 2027 (a Saturday; CDTFA treats a return received on Monday 2 August 2027 as on time). USD 100 of extra sales across the line made the quarter go from nil to USD 555.50.

```oa-check
name: CFET small vendor either side of USD 5,000, with excess reimbursement
inputs:
  q1_gross: 4950
  q2_gross: 5050
  rate: 0.11
  threshold: 5000
  q1_reimbursement_charged: 544.50
steps:
  - q1_taxable_quarter: 1 - min(1, ceil(max(0, threshold - q1_gross)))
  - q2_taxable_quarter: 1 - min(1, ceil(max(0, threshold - q2_gross)))
  - q1_cfet: round(q1_gross * rate * q1_taxable_quarter, 2)
  - q2_cfet: round(q2_gross * rate * q2_taxable_quarter, 2)
  - q1_excess_reimbursement: q1_reimbursement_charged - q1_cfet
  - q1_pay_if_refunded: q1_cfet
  - q1_pay_if_kept: q1_cfet + q1_excess_reimbursement
  - cliff_jump: q2_cfet - q1_cfet
expected:
  q1_taxable_quarter: 0
  q2_taxable_quarter: 1
  q1_cfet: 0.00
  q2_cfet: 555.50
  q1_excess_reimbursement: 544.50
  q1_pay_if_refunded: 0.00
  q1_pay_if_kept: 544.50
  cliff_jump: 555.50
```

## When to refuse or refer

- **What an item is.** Whether something is a firearm, firearm precursor part or ammunition under the Penal Code: refer to DOJ's Bureau of Firearms; for taxability, CDTFA.
- **Validity of the tax.** Constitutional or other challenges: refer.
- **Sales tax computation and local rates.** Use california-sales-tax and CDTFA.
- **Federal excise tax, local taxes, DOJ and ATF licensing.** Not covered here.
- **Penalty relief, refunds, determinations and appeals.** Refer to CDTFA under the Fee Collection Procedures Law.

## Filing and payment

- **Registration:** CFET certificate of registration through CDTFA Online Services; separate from the seller's permit; no fee (R&TC 36035; L-953).
- **Return:** California Firearm and Ammunition Excise Tax Return, quarterly, filed electronically (R&TC 36033). Lines in CDTFA's instructions: total gross receipts; exemption for sales to peace officers and law enforcement agencies; gross receipts subject to tax; rate; CFET due; and "Excess CFET reimbursement collected" (CDTFA CFET return).
- **Due:** on or before the last day of the month after the quarter (R&TC 36032). See the table near the top for the 2026 and 2027 dates.
- **Payment:** through CDTFA Online Services; EFT if CDTFA requires it (average USD 20,000 a month or more, R&TC 55050), initiated by the due date and before 3:00 p.m. Pacific time (CDTFA Getting Started).
- **Extensions and relief:** requested online (CDTFA Getting Started; R&TC 55044).
- **Records:** at least four years: sales records by date of title or possession transfer, separately stated charges and state fees, CDTFA-230-FET certificates, resale certificates, consignment and transfer invoices (CDTFA Getting Started).

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Seller type confirmed; CFET certificate of registration and seller's permit in place.
- [ ] Quarter's retail sales listed by date of transfer of title or possession, whichever came first (possession for firearms after the waiting period); resales on valid certificates left out.
- [ ] Separately stated card, handling, transfer, fabrication and shipping charges included; DROS and other state fees, sales tax and CFET reimbursement excluded.
- [ ] Peace officer and law enforcement sales supported by CDTFA-230-FET and deducted.
- [ ] USD 5,000 quarterly test applied; open points on its scope noted.
- [ ] Transfers for out-of-state retailers and consignments classified.
- [ ] Excess CFET reimbursement refunded or reported.
- [ ] Return filed online and CFET paid by the due date; separate sales and use tax return filed.
