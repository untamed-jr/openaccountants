# California Lumber Products Assessment (1%) v0.1

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from Public Resources Code (PRC) 4629.3, 4629.4 and 4629.5 (current text read in the official California Legislature bulk data archive, with the CDTFA Business Taxes Law Guide copy) and CDTFA Regulations 2000 and 2001; CDTFA's Tax Guide for California Lumber Products Assessment (overview, Retailers, Purchasers, Contractors, Industry Topics, Definitions and Resources pages); CDTFA flyer L-403; and CDTFA's special taxes and fees rate table. All were read on 2026-11-23 (California, Pacific time). No accountant has reviewed it.

**Answer first.** "There is hereby imposed an assessment on a person who purchases a lumber product or an engineered wood product for storage, use, or other consumption in this state, at the rate of 1 percent of the sales price" (PRC 4629.5(a)(1)). The purchaser owes it; the retailer must charge it as a separate charge, show it separately on the receipt, collect it at the sale and pay it to CDTFA (PRC 4629.5(a)(2), (a)(3), (b)). Retailers "Report and pay the lumber products assessment ... on their sales and use tax return", on its lumber schedule, filed electronically ([CDTFA Retailers](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/california-lumber-products-assessment/retailers.htm)). The assessment "is not subject to sales or use tax" (same page).

**Low-volume retailers.** "A retailer with de minimis sales of qualified lumber products and engineered wood products of less than twenty-five thousand dollars ($25,000) during the previous calendar year is not a retailer for purposes of this section" (PRC 4629.5(g)(2)(B)). CDTFA: such a retailer is "not required to collect the lumber products assessment for the subsequent calendar year", counts sales from all locations, must tell customers to pay CDTFA directly (flyer L-403), and may still collect voluntarily; anything it does collect must be paid over ([CDTFA Retailers](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/california-lumber-products-assessment/retailers.htm)). Its customers, and anyone who buys from outside California for use here, pay the 1% directly to CDTFA ([CDTFA Purchasers](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/california-lumber-products-assessment/purchasers.htm); [CDTFA Contractors](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/california-lumber-products-assessment/contractors.htm)).

**Tax year.** Tax year label 2026-27. The rate has been 1% since 1 January 2013 ([CDTFA special taxes and fees rates](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/special-taxes-and-fees-tax-rates/)). PRC 4629.5 was last amended by Stats. 2014, Ch. 810 (AB 2031), effective 1 January 2015, which added the low-volume retailer rule, per both the archive's section history and the Law Guide. Among 141 bills chaptered in 2026 on forestry, timber, lumber, wood, budget, resources, fees, taxes and fire, the only one that amends this article is AB 706 (approved 30 September 2026, with no urgency or immediate-effect clause). Its sections amend PRC 4629.6 and 4629.8 (the uses and priorities of the fund) and add a new forestry article; it does not amend PRC 4629.5. No change to the assessment for 2027 was found as of the read date. PRC 4629.5, as read, contains no sunset date.

**Circular 230 disclosure.** This material is general information, not written advice on a specific transaction under 31 CFR 10.37. It is not intended to be used, and cannot be used, to avoid penalties.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope: retailers of lumber and engineered wood products (lumber yards, home centers, marketplace facilitators, contractors that sell at retail), construction contractors and other purchasers who must pay CDTFA directly, and their bookkeepers. The job: decide which products and sales carry the 1%, compute it, and report and pay it.

Out of scope, and where it goes:

| Topic | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Sales and use tax on the same sale | california-sales-tax |
| Whether a specific product is a "lumber product" or "engineered wood product" | Board of Forestry and Fire Protection regulation and guidance (not read); CDTFA's list below |
| Timber yield tax, timber harvest plans | refer |
| Use of the Timber Regulation and Forest Restoration Fund (PRC 4629.6, 4629.8, AB 706) | not covered |
| Startup cost reimbursement claims for locations registered before 2013 | summarized only; refer to CDTFA |

## Ask the client first

- Is the business a retailer of lumber or engineered wood products, a contractor, or another purchaser? Does its sales and use tax account have the lumber schedule?
- What were its sales of qualifying lumber and engineered wood products in the previous calendar year, across all locations (the USD 25,000 test)?
- For the period: what were the retail sales of qualifying products, with separately stated delivery and handling charges? Which sales were for resale, shipped directly outside California, to the U.S. Government, or to American Indians in Indian country?
- Were any products returned for a refund, or exchanged?
- Did it buy qualifying products for use in California from out-of-state sellers or from low-volume retailers that did not collect the 1%?
- Does it sell kits, furniture, doors, cabinets, trusses or other processed products?

### Steps

1. **Is the product in?** "Lumber product" means a product in which wood or wood fiber is a principal component part, "including, but not limited to, a solid wood product, or an engineered wood product, that is identified in regulations adopted by the board"; it "does not include furniture, paper products, indoor flooring products such as hardwood or laminated flooring, bark or cork products, firewood, or other products not typically regarded as lumber products" (PRC 4629.3(b)(5)). The Board of Forestry identifies the products by regulation and updates it each year (PRC 4629.4(a)). CDTFA's list ([CDTFA Definitions](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/california-lumber-products-assessment/definitions.htm), "current as of July 31, 2014"):
   - **In:** all grades and dimensions of lumber, structural panels, decking, railings, fencing, poles, shakes and wooden shingles, siding, lath, subflooring, railroad ties, recycled or salvaged lumber; plywood, oriented strand board, particleboard, fiberboard, glulam, LVL, I-joists, hardboard; gypsum-, cement- and ceramic-bonded composites and "wood thermoplastic composite materials including plastic lumber and decking".
   - **Out:** furniture, doors, windows, cabinets, molding, millwork, indoor finished flooring, trusses, pre-constructed fencing and railing sections, wood pallets, drywall, ladders, tools, firewood, and kits "usually precut, predrilled, or manufactured, and then disassembled for shipment" (deck packages, shed kits).
2. **Is the sale subject to it?** It applies at the retail sale; acquiring inventory for resale does not trigger it ([CDTFA Industry Topics](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/california-lumber-products-assessment/industry-topics.htm)). "In general, the lumber products assessment is not due on" sales for resale, sales "shipped directly to customers outside of California", and "Sales to the U.S. Government"; sales involving Native Americans follow the sales and use tax rules ([CDTFA Retailers](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/california-lumber-products-assessment/retailers.htm)).
3. **Is the retailer required to collect this year?** Compare its prior calendar year's qualifying sales, all locations, with USD 25,000. Under: no duty to collect; hand customers flyer L-403; may collect voluntarily. USD 25,000 or more: collect from 1 January of the following year (CDTFA Retailers).
4. **Measure the sales price.** "Sales price" has the sales tax meaning (PRC 4629.5(g)(3)). "In general, the sales price does not include separately stated delivery charges or sales tax. However, handling charges are considered part of the sales price" (CDTFA Industry Topics).
5. **Charge and show it.** The retailer "shall separately state the amount of the assessment" on the sales receipt (PRC 4629.5(b)); CDTFA accepts labels such as "lumber products assessment, LPA, lumber assessment, lumber fee or lumber" (CDTFA Retailers). Do not include it in the sales tax measure.
6. **Returns, exchanges and excess.** The assessment "is refundable to the customer on merchandise that is returned"; an even exchange carries no additional assessment; an amount collected above the assessment must be returned to the customer or paid to CDTFA (CDTFA Industry Topics; PRC 4629.5(d)(1)).
7. **Purchasers and contractors who pay directly.** A contractor that consumes lumber in construction pays the 1% to California vendors, or directly to CDTFA on purchases from outside California and from low-volume retailers that do not collect; a contractor that sells lumber at retail collects it; a contractor that is "a retailer of items that you manufacture, assemble, process, or produce from lumber products or engineered wood products (for example, prefabricated cabinets)" pays it neither on its purchase nor on its sale (CDTFA Contractors). There is no exemption for fixed price contracts (CDTFA Contractors). Purchasers without a seller's permit or use tax account use CDTFA's "One-Time Use Tax and/or Lumber Return" (CDTFA Purchasers).
8. **File and pay.** The assessment "is due and payable ... quarterly on or before the last day of the month next succeeding each quarterly period", with an electronic return (PRC 4629.5(f)). In practice retailers report it on the lumber schedule of the sales and use tax return; ask CDTFA to add the schedule if it is missing (CDTFA Retailers). It is administered under the Fee Collection Procedures Law (PRC 4629.5(c)).

**AUDIT FLASH POINT** (the product line and the USD 25,000 test). Whether a product is a lumber product turns on the Board of Forestry's regulation; CDTFA's list dates from 2014 and the Board updates its regulation each year. Processed items (molding with profiling, pre-assembled sections, kits) are out, but "the addition of any value to a lumber product does not automatically result in a product not subject to the assessment" (CDTFA Definitions). Retailers near USD 25,000 that stop collecting, multi-location retailers that test each location separately, and contractors that buy out of state without paying directly are the usual audit points. CDTFA, to a retailer required to collect that did not: "you are still responsible for the payment of the assessment to CDTFA" (CDTFA Retailers).

## Worked cases

Both cases are made up for illustration.

**Case 1: a lumber yard's October to December 2026 quarter.**

- **Facts:** a lumber yard with two locations, USD 2.1 million of qualifying sales in 2025, so it must collect in 2026. Its startup reimbursement was used up years ago. In the quarter:
  - Total sales of qualifying lumber and panels (retail and resale), including USD 3,000 of handling charges: USD 412,000.
  - Separately stated delivery charges: USD 9,500.
  - Sales of qualifying products to other dealers for resale: USD 60,000.
  - Lumber shipped directly to customers in Arizona: USD 15,000.
  - Sales to a U.S. Government agency: USD 7,000.
  - Doors, cabinets and molding: USD 48,000 (not lumber products).
  - Refunds for returned lumber sold earlier in the quarter: USD 4,000.
- **Base:** of the USD 412,000 of total qualifying sales (handling included), take out the resales, the Arizona shipments, the federal sales and the refunds: 412,000 - 60,000 - 15,000 - 7,000 - 4,000 = USD 326,000. Delivery charges and the doors, cabinets and molding were never in it.
- **Assessment:** 1% x 326,000 = **USD 3,260**, reported on the lumber schedule and due 31 January 2027 (a Sunday; see Filing and payment). Sales tax is computed separately, on a measure that excludes the assessment.

```oa-check
name: Lumber products assessment, lumber yard, October to December 2026
inputs:
  qualifying_sales_incl_handling: 412000
  delivery_charges_separately_stated: 9500
  resale_sales: 60000
  shipped_out_of_state: 15000
  us_government_sales: 7000
  non_lumber_products: 48000
  refunds_returned_lumber: 4000
  rate: 0.01
steps:
  - base: qualifying_sales_incl_handling - resale_sales - shipped_out_of_state - us_government_sales - refunds_returned_lumber
  - assessment: round(base * rate, 2)
expected:
  base: 326000
  assessment: 3260.00
```

**Case 2: a small hardware store near USD 25,000, and its contractor customer.**

- **Facts:** a single-location hardware store had USD 24,800 of qualifying lumber sales in 2026 and USD 25,300 in 2027. A contractor buys USD 3,000 of lumber from it in 2027 and USD 20,000 of lumber from a Nevada seller that does not collect the assessment, for a California job.
- **2027:** 24,800 is less than 25,000, so the store is not required to collect in 2027. It gives the contractor flyer L-403. If the store does not collect, the contractor pays CDTFA directly: 1% x (3,000 + 20,000) = **USD 230**, plus use tax on the Nevada purchase. If the store collects voluntarily as a courtesy, USD 30 goes through the store (which reports and pays what it collects) and the contractor pays only USD 200 directly.
- **2028:** 25,300 is not under 25,000, so the store must collect from 1 January 2028.
- **The decision for the store in 2027:** collecting voluntarily costs the store nothing in assessment (it passes the 1% to customers) but keeps the lumber schedule on its return; not collecting removes that work but pushes the filing onto each customer.

```oa-check
name: Lumber products assessment, low-volume retailer test and direct payment
inputs:
  sales_2026: 24800
  sales_2027: 25300
  threshold: 25000
  contractor_purchase_from_store: 3000
  contractor_purchase_nevada: 20000
  rate: 0.01
steps:
  - store_must_collect_2027: 1 - min(1, ceil(max(0, threshold - sales_2026)))
  - store_must_collect_2028: 1 - min(1, ceil(max(0, threshold - sales_2027)))
  - contractor_direct_if_store_does_not_collect: round((contractor_purchase_from_store * (1 - store_must_collect_2027) + contractor_purchase_nevada) * rate, 2)
  - through_store_if_store_collects: round(contractor_purchase_from_store * rate, 2)
  - contractor_direct_if_store_collects: round(contractor_purchase_nevada * rate, 2)
  - total_either_way: through_store_if_store_collects + contractor_direct_if_store_collects
expected:
  store_must_collect_2027: 0
  store_must_collect_2028: 1
  contractor_direct_if_store_does_not_collect: 230.00
  through_store_if_store_collects: 30.00
  contractor_direct_if_store_collects: 200.00
  total_either_way: 230.00
```

## When to refuse or refer

- **Product classification.** Whether a product is subject to the assessment: the Board of Forestry's regulation and guidance; CDTFA for application.
- **Sales and use tax.** Use california-sales-tax.
- **Startup reimbursement and old refund claims.** Refer to CDTFA (Regulations 2000 and 2001).
- **Penalties, refunds, determinations and appeals.** Refer to CDTFA under the Fee Collection Procedures Law.

## Filing and payment

- **Registration:** a person required to pay the assessment registers with CDTFA (PRC 4629.5(h)). When registering online, answering yes to selling or buying lumber products adds the lumber schedule to the return (CDTFA Contractors).
- **Return:** the lumber schedule of the electronic sales and use tax return; purchasers without an account use the "One-Time Use Tax and/or Lumber Return" (CDTFA Purchasers).
- **Due:** quarterly, by the last day of the month after the quarter (PRC 4629.5(f)): for October to December 2026, 31 January 2027, a Sunday. No weekend or holiday rule for the assessment was read; file and pay by the Friday before, or confirm with CDTFA.
- **Late:** the Fee Collection Procedures Law applies (PRC 4629.5(c)); the penalty and interest rules were not read for this skill.
- **Records:** prior-year qualifying sales by location (for the USD 25,000 test), product classifications, delivery and handling charges, resale certificates, out-of-state shipping records and refunds.

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Prior-year qualifying sales, all locations, compared with USD 25,000.
- [ ] Each product classified against CDTFA's list and the Board of Forestry regulation; kits and processed items out.
- [ ] Handling in; separately stated delivery and sales tax out.
- [ ] Resales, direct out-of-state shipments and U.S. Government sales excluded.
- [ ] Assessment shown separately on receipts; refunds on returns passed back.
- [ ] Lumber schedule on the return; assessment reported and paid by the due date.
- [ ] Contractor and purchaser direct payments made where the seller did not collect.
