# California timeliness penalties and One-Time Penalty Abatement (2026) v0.1

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from Revenue and Taxation Code (R&TC) 19131, 19132 and 19132.5, read in the official California Legislature bulk data archive; the Franchise Tax Board (FTB) pages One-Time Penalty Abatement, Help with penalties and fees, Claim for refund, Interest and estimate penalty rates and Due dates (personal); the 2025 Form 540 booklet (line 112, Interest and Penalties); FTB 2918 (One-Time Penalty Abatement, Individual), FTB 2917 (Reasonable Cause, Individual and Fiduciary Claim for Refund) and FTB 2924 (business entity version); and FTB 1024, Penalty Reference Chart (revised 1/2026). All were read on 2026-11-23 (California, Pacific time). No accountant has reviewed it.

**Answer first.** An individual who filed or paid a California personal income tax return late can ask FTB to cancel the late filing penalty (R&TC 19131) and the late payment penalty (R&TC 19132) in one of two ways:

- **One-Time Penalty Abatement** (R&TC 19132.5, from AB 194): no reason needed. FTB describes it as "A once-in-a-lifetime abatement", "Available only to individuals subject to Personal Income Tax Law", "Not applicable to fiduciaries, estates or trusts" and "Applicable for taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2022" ([FTB One-Time Penalty Abatement](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/pay/penalties-and-interest/one-time-penalty-abatement.html)). The penalty "shall be abated" if the taxpayer has not previously been granted it, has filed all required returns as of the request, and has paid, or is current on an installment agreement for, all other tax, penalties, fees and interest for those returns (R&TC 19132.5(b)). Request it "either orally or in writing" (R&TC 19132.5(b)): by phone, MyFTB chat or message, or FTB 2918.
- **Reasonable cause:** both penalties are not imposed if "the failure is due to reasonable cause and not due to willful neglect" (R&TC 19131(a); 19132(a)(1)). FTB: "Reasonable cause means that the act occurred despite the exercise of ordinary business care and prudence" ([FTB Claim for refund](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/help/disagree-or-resolve-an-issue/claim-for-refund.html)). Claim on FTB 2917 (individuals and fiduciaries) or FTB 2924 (business entities); FTB "cannot act on your claim for refund until your balance due for that tax year or income period is paid in full" (FTB 2917 instructions).

A taxpayer may ask for reasonable cause first and, if refused, then use the one-time abatement, or skip straight to the one-time abatement (R&TC 19132.5(a)). A penalty removed for reasonable cause does not use up the one-time abatement, because the test in R&TC 19132.5(b)(1) counts only abatement "granted ... under this section". California has no federal-style first-time abatement based on filing history: FTB 2917 says "California does not conform to federal relief from penalties based upon good filing history or First-Time Abatement", but FTB "may abate a penalty if you can provide IRS documentation, which clearly states that the IRS abated the same penalty for 'reasonable cause.'" The one-time abatement is California's own rule.

**Tax year.** For the 2025 Form 540, due 15 April 2026, FTB "grants an automatic extension until October 15, 2026 to file your return, although your payment is still due by April 15, 2026" ([FTB due dates, personal](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/when-to-file/due-dates-personal.html)). The underpayment interest rate is 7% for 1 July to 31 December 2026 ([FTB interest rates](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/pay/penalties-and-interest/interest-and-estimate-penalty-rates.html)). In the official archive, R&TC 19131 was last amended in 2010, 19132 in 1998 and 19132.5 was repealed and added by AB 194 (Stats. 2022, Ch. 55); none of the 1,263 bills chaptered in 2026 names R&TC 19131, 19132 or 19132.5 in its title.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope: individuals (Form 540, 540NR, 540 2EZ) and their preparers dealing with FTB late filing and late payment penalties, and fiduciaries using reasonable cause. The job: work out the penalty, decide which relief route fits, and make the request on time.

Out of scope, and where it goes:

| Topic | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Underpayment of estimated tax penalty (R&TC 19136; FTB 5805) | ca-estimated-tax-540es; not eligible for the one-time abatement |
| Interest abatement (FTB 3701), Taxpayer Advocate equity relief (FTB 3705), mandatory e-pay waiver (FTB 4107) | FTB 2917 instructions list these; not covered here |
| Business entity penalties (corporations, LLCs, partnerships) | FTB 2924 for reasonable cause; ca-llc-fee-and-tax and the business skills |
| Failure to file after demand (R&TC 19133), accuracy and fraud penalties | FTB 1024; not covered here |
| Withholding penalties (Forms 592, 593) | not covered here; FTB 2917 sends Form 592 and 593 requests to Withholding Services and Compliance |
| Computing the return itself | ca-540-individual-return |

## Ask the client first

- Which taxable year, which form (540, 540NR, 540 2EZ), and is the taxpayer an individual, or a fiduciary for an estate or trust?
- When was the return filed, and when was each payment made? What was paid by the original due date (withholding, estimates, extension payment)?
- What penalties are on the FTB notice, and under which sections?
- Has the taxpayer, or either spouse or registered domestic partner on a joint return, ever been granted a California one-time abatement before (any year)? Is the penalty on a joint return?
- Are all required California returns filed today? Is every other balance paid, or is there an installment agreement that is current?
- Is there a reason that could be reasonable cause (illness, disaster, reliance on advice, records destroyed)? Did the IRS abate the matching federal penalty for reasonable cause, and is there a letter saying so?
- Has the penalty been paid? If so, when (for the refund deadline)?

### Steps

1. **Identify the penalties.** Read the notice. Only penalties under R&TC 19131 (late filing) and 19132 (late payment) are "timeliness penalties" for the one-time abatement: "'timeliness penalty' means a penalty imposed under Section 19131 or 19132 for one taxable year with respect to a return filed by an individual for that taxable year" (R&TC 19132.5(c)). The estimated tax penalty is outside 19131 (R&TC 19131(e)) and outside the one-time abatement.
2. **Check the late filing penalty.** It applies only if the return was not filed by the due date "or the due date as extended" (R&TC 19131(a)); for the 2025 Form 540, filing by 15 October 2026 avoids it. If it applies, it runs "for each month or fraction thereof elapsing between the due date of the return (determined without regard to any extension of time for filing) and the date on which filed", at 5% a month, up to 25%, on the tax "reduced by the amount of any part of the tax which is paid on or before the date prescribed for payment of the tax and by the amount of any credit" (R&TC 19131(a), (c)). For an individual or fiduciary more than 60 days late (counting the extension), it "may not be less than the lesser of one hundred thirty-five dollars ($135) or 100 percent of the amount of tax required to be shown on the return" (R&TC 19131(b)).
3. **Check the late payment penalty.** It is 5% of the total tax unpaid at the payment due date plus 0.5% a month of the remaining tax "for each additional month or fraction thereof not to exceed 40 months", capped at 25% of the total unpaid tax (R&TC 19132(a)(2), (3)). An extension to file does not extend the time to pay. It "shall not be assessed if, for the same taxable year, the sum of any penalties imposed under Section 19131 ... and Section 19133 ... is equal to or greater than" it; otherwise only the excess is charged (R&TC 19132(b)). FTB's 2025 Form 540 booklet adds: "Reasonable cause is presumed when 90% of the tax shown on the return is paid by the original due date of the return" ([2025 Form 540 booklet](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-540-booklet.pdf), line 112).
4. **Test the one-time abatement.** All must be true (R&TC 19132.5(b), (f); FTB 2918):
   - the taxpayer is an individual (not an estate, trust or business), and the penalty is for a taxable year beginning on or after 1 January 2022;
   - no one-time abatement has been granted before, for any year ("if approved it will not be applicable for any future years regardless of circumstance", FTB 2918);
   - every required California personal income tax return is filed as of the request;
   - all other tax, penalties, fees and interest are paid, or under an installment agreement that is current.
5. **Decide the route.** If there is real reasonable cause, a reasonable cause claim keeps the one-time abatement for a future year, because R&TC 19132.5(b)(1) bars only a taxpayer who has "previously been granted abatement under this section". R&TC 19131(f) presumes the California late filing penalty does not apply when all three of these hold for the same year: the taxpayer filed both the California and the federal return late (after any extension); FTB proposes a deficiency assessment based on a final federal determination; and the IRS determined that the federal late filing penalty does not apply because the late federal filing was due to reasonable cause and not willful neglect. FTB may rebut it by a preponderance of the evidence. Where there is no reasonable cause, use the one-time abatement.
6. **Request it.**
   - **One-time abatement:** call 800-689-4776, use MyFTB chat or a MyFTB message, or file FTB 2918 by mail or MyFTB upload (FTB One-Time Penalty Abatement page). "If you have already paid in full all outstanding liabilities, including the penalty you are seeking to abate, this form will serve as a Claim for Refund" (FTB 2918); FTB's page says a taxpayer who has paid everything, including the penalty, "would need to send a written statement or Form 2918".
   - **Reasonable cause:** file FTB 2917 (one per taxpayer and per tax year) with "a detailed explanation" and supporting documents, by mail or MyFTB; pay the balance first (FTB 2917 instructions; FTB Claim for refund).
7. **Watch the deadline.** A refund claim must be filed by the latest of "Four years after the original tax return due date", "Four years after the date of a timely filed tax return" or "One year from the date of overpayment" (FTB 2918; FTB 2917). A claim "will not automatically stop collection action" (FTB Claim for refund).
8. **Interest is separate.** FTB's booklet: "Interest will be charged on any late filing or late payment penalty from the original due date of the return to the date paid", and "the imposition of interest is mandatory" ([2025 Form 540 booklet](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-540-booklet.pdf), line 112). No interest accrues on the 0.5% monthly part of the late payment penalty (R&TC 19132(f)). Interest abatement is a separate request on FTB 3701 (FTB 2917 instructions).

**AUDIT FLASH POINT** (using the one-time abatement on the wrong year, or not at all). Because it is once in a lifetime, using it on a small penalty can leave nothing for a larger one later; a reasonable cause claim that succeeds does not use it up. FTB grants it only if every return is filed and every other balance is paid or on a current installment agreement, so a request made while another year is unfiled will fail. FTB 2917 says California does not follow the federal first-time abatement; preparers who assume a clean IRS history carries over are relying on the wrong rule.

## Boundary and exception table

| Situation | Result | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2025 return filed 10 October 2026, tax paid in full 15 April 2026 | No late filing or late payment penalty | R&TC 19131(a); FTB due dates |
| 2025 return filed 10 October 2026, balance paid with the return | Late payment penalty only (extension covers filing, not payment), unless 90% of the tax shown was paid by 15 April 2026 (reasonable cause presumed) | R&TC 19132(a); FTB due dates; 2025 Form 540 booklet |
| Late filing penalty USD 1,000 and late payment penalty would be USD 600 | Late payment penalty not assessed | R&TC 19132(b) |
| Individual with no prior one-time abatement, all returns filed, other balances paid | One-time abatement of the timeliness penalty for the year requested (whether one request clears both penalties: see Not yet confirmed) | R&TC 19132.5(b), (c) |
| Taxpayer granted one-time abatement for 2023, now late for 2025 | Not available again; reasonable cause only | R&TC 19132.5(b)(1); FTB 2918 |
| Trust or estate (Form 541) late | No one-time abatement; reasonable cause on FTB 2917 | FTB One-Time Penalty Abatement; FTB 2917 |
| Penalty for taxable year 2021 | No one-time abatement (before 2022) | R&TC 19132.5(f) |
| Estimated tax penalty on Form 5805 | Not a timeliness penalty; not abated by one-time abatement | R&TC 19131(e); 19132.5(c) |
| IRS removed the federal penalty under its first-time abatement | Does not carry over; reasonable cause documentation from the IRS can support a California claim | FTB 2917 instructions |
| Earlier penalty was abated for reasonable cause | Does not count as a prior one-time abatement | R&TC 19132.5(d)(1) |
| 2022 return still unfiled when asking for one-time abatement on 2024 | Not eligible until all required returns are filed | R&TC 19132.5(b)(2) |

## Worked cases

Both cases are made up for illustration.

**Case 1: a 2025 return filed after the extension, and the one-time abatement.**

- **Facts:** an individual's 2025 Form 540 shows tax of USD 10,000 after credits. USD 6,000 was withheld and paid by 15 April 2026, leaving USD 4,000. The return and the USD 4,000 were filed and paid on Friday 20 November 2026, after the 15 October 2026 extended date but within 60 days of it (the 60th day is 14 December 2026). The taxpayer has never been granted a one-time abatement, has filed every other return and owes nothing else.
- **Late filing penalty:** 15 April to 20 November 2026 is 7 full months plus a fraction, so 8 months: 8 x 5% = 40%, capped at 25%. 25% x 4,000 = **USD 1,000**. The 60-day minimum does not apply.
- **Late payment penalty:** capped at 25% x 4,000 = USD 1,000, which is not more than the USD 1,000 late filing penalty, so none is assessed (R&TC 19132(b)), whatever the month count.
- **Relief:** with no reasonable cause, the taxpayer calls FTB or files FTB 2918 for taxable year 2025. All conditions are met, so the USD 1,000 is abated. Only 60% of the tax was paid by 15 April 2026, so the 90% reasonable cause presumption for late payment would not have helped. Interest on the late tax is not part of this request (see step 8).

```oa-check
name: Late filing and late payment penalties, 2025 Form 540 filed 20 November 2026, one-time abatement
inputs:
  tax_after_credits: 10000
  paid_by_due_date: 6000
  months_late_filing: 8
  monthly_rate: 0.05
  cap: 0.25
  minimum_flat: 135
  more_than_60_days_late: 0
  ota_eligible: 1
steps:
  - unpaid_tax: tax_after_credits - paid_by_due_date
  - late_filing_rate: min(cap, months_late_filing * monthly_rate)
  - late_filing_basic: round(unpaid_tax * late_filing_rate, 2)
  - late_filing_minimum: min(minimum_flat, unpaid_tax) * more_than_60_days_late
  - late_filing_penalty: max(late_filing_basic, late_filing_minimum)
  - late_payment_max: round(unpaid_tax * cap, 2)
  - late_payment_assessed: max(0, late_payment_max - late_filing_penalty)
  - total_penalties: late_filing_penalty + late_payment_assessed
  - after_abatement: total_penalties * (1 - ota_eligible)
expected:
  unpaid_tax: 4000
  late_filing_rate: 0.25
  late_filing_basic: 1000.00
  late_filing_minimum: 0
  late_filing_penalty: 1000.00
  late_payment_max: 1000.00
  late_payment_assessed: 0
  total_penalties: 1000.00
  after_abatement: 0
```

**Case 2: the minimum penalty, and no second abatement.**

- **Facts:** a different individual, granted a one-time abatement for taxable year 2023, files a 2025 Form 540 showing tax of USD 600 with USD 510 withheld (85%, so no 90% presumption), leaving USD 90 unpaid. The return and the USD 90 are filed and paid on 1 February 2027, more than 60 days after the 15 October 2026 extended date (the 60th day was 14 December 2026).
- **Late filing penalty:** 15 April 2026 to 1 February 2027 is 9 full months plus a fraction, so 10 months and the 25% cap: 25% x 90 = USD 22.50; but the minimum is the lesser of USD 135 or 100% of the USD 90, so **USD 90**.
- **Late payment penalty:** at most 25% x 90 = USD 22.50, less than the USD 90 late filing penalty, so none.
- **Relief:** the one-time abatement was used for 2023, so it is not available. Only a reasonable cause claim on FTB 2917 remains, after paying the balance.

```oa-check
name: Minimum late filing penalty more than 60 days late, one-time abatement already used
inputs:
  unpaid_tax: 90
  months_late_filing: 10
  monthly_rate: 0.05
  cap: 0.25
  minimum_flat: 135
  more_than_60_days_late: 1
  ota_previously_granted: 1
steps:
  - late_filing_basic: round(unpaid_tax * min(cap, months_late_filing * monthly_rate), 2)
  - late_filing_minimum: min(minimum_flat, unpaid_tax) * more_than_60_days_late
  - late_filing_penalty: max(late_filing_basic, late_filing_minimum)
  - late_payment_assessed: max(0, round(unpaid_tax * cap, 2) - late_filing_penalty)
  - ota_available: 1 - ota_previously_granted
  - penalty_after_ota: (late_filing_penalty + late_payment_assessed) * (1 - ota_available)
expected:
  late_filing_basic: 22.50
  late_filing_minimum: 90
  late_filing_penalty: 90
  late_payment_assessed: 0
  ota_available: 0
  penalty_after_ota: 90
```

## When to refuse or refer

- **Contested penalties beyond 19131 and 19132**, accuracy, fraud or demand penalties: refer.
- **Protests and appeals** to the Office of Tax Appeals after a denied claim: refer.
- **Business entities**: reasonable cause on FTB 2924; the one-time abatement is not available; refer to the business skills.
- **Hardship, collections and installment agreements**: refer to FTB collections and the Taxpayer Advocate.

## Filing and payment

- **One-time abatement:** phone 800-689-4776 (individuals say "Individual"; representatives say "POA"), MyFTB chat or message, or FTB 2918 mailed to One-Time Penalty Abatement Requests, Franchise Tax Board, PO Box 2952, Sacramento CA 95812-2952, or uploaded in MyFTB (FTB One-Time Penalty Abatement page; FTB 2918).
- **Reasonable cause:** FTB 2917 to General Abatement Requests, Franchise Tax Board, PO Box 942840, Sacramento CA 94240-0040 (Form 592 and 593 requests go to Withholding Services and Compliance), or upload in MyFTB (FTB 2917; FTB Claim for refund).
- **Representatives:** a power of attorney on FTB 3520 PIT, sent separately (FTB 2917; FTB 2918).
- **Pay first** for a reasonable cause claim; a paid penalty is recovered as a refund within the statute of limitations.

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Penalties on the notice identified by section; estimated tax penalty separated out.
- [ ] Late filing penalty recomputed from the original due date, with the extension and the 60-day minimum checked.
- [ ] Late payment penalty reduced by the late filing penalty.
- [ ] One-time abatement conditions checked: individual, year 2022 or later, never granted before, all returns filed, other balances paid or on a current installment agreement.
- [ ] Reasonable cause considered first if it exists, to keep the one-time abatement.
- [ ] Request made by phone, MyFTB or FTB 2918, or FTB 2917 after payment, within the refund deadline.
