# California nonresident and backup withholding, Form 592 (2026) v0.1

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from Revenue and Taxation Code (R&TC) 18662, 18664, 18668 and 19183, read in the official California Legislature bulk data archive; the Franchise Tax Board (FTB) 2026 Instructions for Form 592, 2026 Forms 592, 592-B (with instructions), 592-V and 590, the 2026 Instructions for Form 588, FTB Publication 1017 (revised 02/2026), FTB 1150 (revised 02-2026) and FTB's backup withholding page. All were read on 2026-11-23 (California, Pacific time). The FTB regulations under R&TC 18662 (California Code of Regulations, title 18, section 18662-0 onward) were not read: the host that publishes them refused automated access. The 7% nonresident rate and the USD 1,500 threshold below come from FTB's instructions and Pub. 1017, which apply those regulations. No accountant has reviewed it.

**Answer first.** A business, government body, nonprofit or individual that pays California source income to a nonresident (a "withholding agent") withholds **7%** of the gross California source payment and sends it to FTB, unless the payee is exempt, FTB has waived or reduced it, or the year's payments to that payee are USD 1,500 or less. FTB's instructions: "Withholding, excluding backup withholding, is optional, at the discretion of the withholding agent, on the first $1,500 in payments made during the calendar year. Withholding must begin as soon as the total payments of California source income for the calendar year exceed $1,500" ([2026 Form 592 instructions](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2026/2026-592-instructions.html)). The common cases: payments to nonresident independent contractors for services performed in California, rent on California property paid in the course of the payer's business, royalties from California natural resources, and payments to nonresident entertainers and athletes for California work. Separately, "payers that are required to withhold and remit backup withholding to the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) are also required to withhold and remit to the FTB on income sourced to California", with certain limited exceptions, at 7% of the reportable payment (2026 Form 592 instructions; R&TC 18664(b)). Form 592 "is also used to report withholding payments for a resident payee", and backup withholding "supersedes all types of withholding" and has "no reductions or waivers" and "no set minimum threshold" (2026 Form 592 instructions).

The agent reports each period on **Form 592** (with its Schedule of Payees), pays with **Form 592-V**, and gives each payee a **Form 592-B** by 31 January of the next year. If it does not withhold, it is liable for "the amount actually withheld, or the amount of taxes due from the taxpayer to whom the payments are made, whichever is greater, but not in excess of the amount required to be withheld", plus interest, unless the failure was due to reasonable cause (R&TC 18668(a), (b)).

## Scope and who this is for

In scope: any payer of California source income to nonresident payees, as reported on Form 592, and any payer of reportable payments subject to California backup withholding, and their bookkeepers and advisers. The job: decide whether to withhold, how much, and when and how to report and pay.

Out of scope, and where it goes:

| Topic | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Withholding by partnerships, LLCs, S corporations, estates and trusts on distributions to their domestic nonresident owners (Forms 592-PTE and 592-Q) | not covered here; FTB Form 592-PTE instructions |
| Withholding on foreign partners or members (R&TC 18666; Forms 592-F and 592-A) | not covered here |
| Real estate withholding (Form 593) | not covered here; FTB Form 593 instructions |
| Wages paid to employees | ca-payroll; the Employment Development Department |
| The nonresident payee's own California return and residency questions | ca-540-individual-return; ca-residency-leaving-california |
| Elective withholding boxes on Form 592 (including by an Indian tribe) | not covered here |

Note on Form 592's own text: its Purpose paragraph still lists "distributions to domestic nonresident partners in a partnership, members of an LLC, estate or trust beneficiaries, and S corporation shareholders" among items reported, while the same instructions say not to use Form 592 for "withholding on domestic nonresident individuals, who are owners of the PTE" (use Form 592-PTE). Pub. 1017 says: "When an entity is a PTE (LLC, S Corp, Partnership, Estate, or Trust), use Form 592-PTE and Form 592-Q." This skill follows the narrower reading.

## Ask the client first

- Who is the payee: an individual, corporation, partnership or LLC, trust or estate? What is its address and taxpayer identification number? Did it give a signed Form 590, and does it have a California street address (not a PO box or "in care of")?
- What is being paid for: services, rent, royalties, prizes, endorsements, goods, or a mix? Where were the services performed?
- How much has been paid to this payee so far in the calendar year, and how much more is expected?
- Has the payee sent a Form 587 allocation, or an FTB waiver (Form 588) or reduced withholding approval (FTB 3952 after Form 589)?
- Is the payee subject to federal backup withholding (no TIN, invalid TIN, or failure to certify)?
- Are any reimbursed expenses separately accounted for and not subject to Form 1099 reporting?
- Is the payer itself a business renting the property, or a residential tenant? Is a property manager involved?

### Steps

1. **Backup withholding first, for every payee.** If federal backup withholding applies (no TIN, an invalid TIN, or failure to certify), withhold 7% of the reportable payment "on income sourced to California" (2026 Form 592 instructions), resident or nonresident, before looking at residency, Form 590 or the USD 1,500 threshold. FTB's backup page takes the exempt payees from the federal Form W-9 list ("Certain payees are exempt, such as government entities and tax-exempt organizations"), not from Form 590 ([FTB backup withholding](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/pay/withholding/backup-withholding.html)). Backup withholding replaces any other California withholding: "Where withholding under both this section and other provisions of this article would otherwise be required, withholding shall only be required under this section" (R&TC 18664(c)). It covers rents, prizes and winnings, compensation for services and other fixed or determinable income, but not "Payment of interest and dividends" or a financial institution's "release of loan funds" (R&TC 18664(a)(2), (3)).
2. **Is the payee a nonresident?** For nonwage withholding, nonresident means "Individuals who are not residents of California", "Corporations not qualified through the California Secretary of State (CA SOS) to do business in California or having no permanent place of business in California", "Partnerships or limited liability companies (LLCs) with no permanent place of business in California" and "Any trust without a resident grantor, beneficiary, or trustee, or estates where the decedent was not a California resident" (2026 Form 592 instructions). The agent may rely on a complete, signed Form 590 (exemption reasons: California resident; corporation with a permanent place of business in California or qualified with the Secretary of State; partnership or LLC with a permanent place of business in California or registered with the Secretary of State; tax-exempt entity; insurance company, IRA or qualified plan; California trust; California estate; nonmilitary spouse under the Military Spouse Residency Relief Act) ([2026 Form 590](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2026/2026-590.pdf)). An incomplete Form 590 "is invalid and the withholding agent should not accept it", and "A withholding agent who knowingly accepts a false Form 590 is subject to the liabilities and penalties related to failure to withhold" (Pub. 1017 Q43, Q46). FTB also accepts a California street address as evidence of residency, but not "a California post office box, or an in care of address" (Pub. 1017 Q36). The Form 590 certification "remains valid until the payee's status changes" (Pub. 1017 Q44). The sources read define two payee types differently: for trusts, the instructions say a trust "without a resident grantor, beneficiary, or trustee", Form 590 certifies that "At least one trustee and one noncontingent beneficiary" is a California resident, and Pub. 1017 Q81 treats a trust as a California trust "if at least one trustee is a California resident"; for corporations, the instructions' "or" reads wider than Form 590 and Pub. 1017 Q22. This skill relies on the payee's Form 590 certification, which protects the agent unless it knows the form is false (Pub. 1017 Q38, Q46).
3. **Is the payment California source income of a kind that is withheld on?** R&TC 18662(b) lists "interest, dividends, rents, prizes and winnings, premiums, annuities, emoluments, compensation for services, including bonuses, partnership income or gains, and other fixed or determinable annual or periodical gains, profits, and income", and FTB requires withholding on the categories it sets (Pub. 1017 Q17):
   - **Services:** "The location where the personal services are performed determines the source of income", not where the payee lives, where the contract was signed or where payment is made. This includes entertainers, covenants not to compete in California, bonuses, seminar providers and expert witnesses (Pub. 1017 Q17, Q79, Q80).
   - **Rent:** on real property or tangible personal property in California, when "Payments are made in the course of the lessee's business"; "tenants of residential property are not required to withhold on payments made directly to nonresident owners, but payments from property management companies are subject to withholding" (Pub. 1017 Q73, Q74). A property manager may deduct its fee before computing the 7% (Pub. 1017 Q75).
   - **Royalties:** for "the right to use natural resources located in California" and royalty or residual payments for services originally performed in California (Pub. 1017 Q76).
   - **Prizes and endorsements:** prizes for contests in California, and endorsement income tied to California events (Pub. 1017 Q17, Q85, Q86).
   - **Not withheld on:** goods; services performed outside California; interest and dividends from intangibles without a California business situs; wages; payments to a nonresident corporate director for director services (R&TC 18662(f)), which instead need an information return and payee statement (R&TC 18662(g)); payees that are banks; motor carriers covered by 49 USC 14503(a)(1); and expense reimbursements that are separately accounted for and "not subject to IRS Form 1099 reporting" (Pub. 1017 Q15, Q20).
4. **Allocate mixed payments.** For work done partly outside California, use "any reasonable method"; the payee can give the agent a signed Form 587, which the agent may rely on unless it "has actual knowledge" it is wrong (Pub. 1017 Q21, Q47, Q48). For personal services, allocate "based on working days in California to total working days in and out of California", the denominator being "the total number of days actually worked on the particular job"; hourly professionals use billable hours (Pub. 1017 Q50). For a sale of goods with services, withhold on the services part, for example using the sales and use tax split in the contract (Pub. 1017 Q23, Q51).
5. **Apply the USD 1,500 threshold.** Withholding is optional on the first USD 1,500 paid to the payee in the calendar year and required "as soon as the total payments of California source income for the calendar year exceed $1,500"; no catch-up is required on earlier payments the agent reasonably expected to stay under the threshold (2026 Form 592 instructions; Pub. 1017 Q11, Q12). For an entertainer, the venue withholds on "the entire contract amount, including any bonuses", deposits notwithstanding: USD 10,000 plus a USD 1,000 bonus means "$770 (7 percent of $11,000)" (Pub. 1017 Q90).
6. **Check for a waiver or reduction.** Honor an FTB Waiver Determination Notice; the agent "must have received the notice authorizing a waiver of withholding before eliminating withholding" (2026 Form 588 instructions). A reduced amount needs FTB 3952 before payment; otherwise "The withholding agent must withhold 7 percent of the gross California source payment" (Pub. 1017 Q60). Form 588 waivers are for domestic nonresident payees and do not apply to backup withholding.
7. **Compute.** Withholding = 7% x the California source payment (after any Form 587 allocation, property management fee, or approved reduction). Backup withholding = 7% x the reportable payment on income sourced to California.
8. **Report and pay each period.** On Form 592, line 1 is the total withholding "excluding backup withholding" from the Schedule of Payees, line 2 is backup withholding, line 3 is the total, lines 4 and 5 take off prior undistributed payments and amounts withheld by another entity, and line 7 is the amount due, remitted "with Form 592-V, along with Form 592" ([2026 Form 592](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2026/2026-592.pdf)); "Do not submit Form 592-V if payment was made electronically" ([2026 Form 592-V](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2026/2026-592-v.pdf)). List each payee with withholding; do not list payees with zero withholding. File online through MyFTB for Withholding Agents or SWIFT, or on paper (SWIFT required over 250 payees).
9. **Give each payee a Form 592-B** by 31 January after the year ([2026 Form 592-B](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2026/2026-592-b.pdf)). The payee claims the credit on its California return; withholding does not remove the payee's filing requirement (Pub. 1017 Q146). Federal treaty exemptions do not carry over: "California does not conform to federal law relating to income protected by U.S. tax treaties" (Pub. 1017 Q19).
10. **Late or missed.** Interest runs from the withholding due date to payment at the adjusted annual rate (R&TC 18668(b)). Amounts withheld are "a special fund in trust for the State of California" (R&TC 18668(c)). Penalties for late or incorrect Forms 592 and 592-B are set by R&TC 19183 through IRC 6721 and 6722, at the amounts in the table above. Penalties may be withdrawn for reasonable cause, and "Penalties are not imposed on withholding agents who make a good faith effort to comply with the law" when an allocation estimate later proves inaccurate (Pub. 1017 Q142, Q144).

**AUDIT FLASH POINT** (where the work was done, and who is a resident). Withholding follows the place the services were performed, so a single contract can be partly withheld on. Agents that pay an out-of-state contractor in full without a Form 587, rely on an unsigned or incomplete Form 590, treat a California PO box or "in care of" address as residency, or skip withholding because the contract says "no withholding" (Pub. 1017 Q91: "California law requires the withholding agent to withhold") carry the payee's tax up to the amount that should have been withheld, plus interest (R&TC 18668(a)). Keep the Form 590 or 587, the payment ledger by payee and calendar year, and the working-day or billable-hour count used for the allocation.

## Worked cases

Both cases are made up for illustration.

**Case 1: a California company pays a Nevada consultant in two installments.**

- **Facts:** a San Diego company hires a sole-proprietor consultant who lives and is based in Nevada, has no Form 590 or FTB waiver, and is not subject to backup withholding. The USD 90,000 fee is paid in two equal installments: USD 45,000 on 10 March 2026 and USD 45,000 on 20 July 2026. The consultant's signed Form 587, Part IV, line 1 (services) shows expected calendar-year payments of USD 60,000 within California (column (a)) and USD 30,000 outside (column (b)), on the basis of 40 of the 60 working days on the job being in California (Pub. 1017 Q50); the same split applies to each installment. The company also reimburses USD 3,000 of travel costs, separately accounted for and not subject to Form 1099 reporting.
- **California source part of each installment:** 45,000 x 40 / 60 = USD 30,000. The first installment alone is over USD 1,500, so withholding applies from the first payment (no catch-up question).
- **Withholding:** 7% x 30,000 = **USD 2,100** on each installment. The reimbursement is not withheld on.
- **Remit:** USD 2,100 with Form 592 and Form 592-V for the 1 January to 31 March period by 15 April 2026, and USD 2,100 for the 1 June to 31 August period by 15 September 2026.
- **Form 592-B by 31 January 2027:** income subject to withholding USD 60,000; tax withheld **USD 4,200**. The consultant receives USD 90,000 - 4,200 + 3,000 = USD 88,800 in cash.

```oa-check
name: Form 592 nonresident consultant, two installments, Form 587 allocation
inputs:
  installment: 45000
  ca_days: 40
  total_days: 60
  reimbursement: 3000
  rate: 0.07
  threshold: 1500
steps:
  - ca_source_each: round(installment * ca_days / total_days, 2)
  - over_threshold: min(1, ceil(max(0, ca_source_each - threshold)))
  - withholding_each: round(ca_source_each * rate * over_threshold, 2)
  - ca_income_592b: ca_source_each * 2
  - total_withheld: withholding_each * 2
  - cash_to_consultant: installment * 2 - total_withheld + reimbursement
expected:
  ca_source_each: 30000
  over_threshold: 1
  withholding_each: 2100.00
  ca_income_592b: 60000
  total_withheld: 4200.00
  cash_to_consultant: 88800.00
```

**Case 2: one Form 592 with nonresident and backup withholding.**

- **Facts:** a Los Angeles retailer, for 1 January to 31 March 2026:
  - Rents store equipment from an Arizona LLC that has no permanent place of business in California and is not registered with the Secretary of State: USD 2,000 a month, January to March. The LLC gave its TIN and no Form 590 or waiver.
  - Pays an Oregon photographer USD 3,000 for a shoot in Los Angeles on 2 February 2026. The photographer never gave a TIN, and the retailer does federal backup withholding on the payment.
- **Equipment rent:** each USD 2,000 payment is over USD 1,500, so withholding applies from January: 7% x 6,000 = USD 420 (USD 140 a month).
- **Photographer:** California backup withholding 7% x 3,000 = USD 210. It replaces nonresident withholding and has no threshold.
- **Form 592 for the period:** line 1 (excluding backup) USD 420; line 2 (backup) USD 210; line 3 total **USD 630**; no prior payments; line 7 due **USD 630** by Wednesday 15 April 2026, with Form 592-V. Two payees on the Schedule of Payees, the photographer marked for backup withholding.

```oa-check
name: Form 592 period with equipment rent withholding and backup withholding
inputs:
  monthly_rent: 2000
  months: 3
  photo_fee: 3000
  rate: 0.07
  backup_rate: 0.07
  threshold: 1500
  prior_payments: 0
  withheld_by_other_entity: 0
steps:
  - rent_total: monthly_rent * months
  - rent_over_threshold: min(1, ceil(max(0, monthly_rent - threshold)))
  - line1_nonresident: round(rent_total * rate * rent_over_threshold, 2)
  - line2_backup: round(photo_fee * backup_rate, 2)
  - line3_total: line1_nonresident + line2_backup
  - line6_payments: prior_payments + withheld_by_other_entity
  - line7_due: line3_total - line6_payments
expected:
  rent_total: 6000
  rent_over_threshold: 1
  line1_nonresident: 420.00
  line2_backup: 210.00
  line3_total: 630.00
  line6_payments: 0
  line7_due: 630.00
```

## When to refuse or refer

- **Pass-through entity distributions, foreign partners, real estate sales, wages.** Use the forms and skills named in the scope table.
- **Residency disputes.** Whether a person is a California resident: refer (FTB Pub. 1031 and the residency skill).
- **Failure-to-withhold assessments, penalty abatement, refunds of over-withholding.** Refer; contact FTB Withholding Services and Compliance.
- **Payee's own return.** The payee claims the credit with Form 592-B on its California return; not covered here.

## Filing and payment

- **Forms:** Form 592 (Resident and Nonresident Withholding Statement) with its Schedule of Payees; Form 592-V (payment voucher); Form 592-B (to each payee, not filed with FTB) (2026 Form 592 instructions).
- **Due dates:** see the table near the top; the weekend or holiday rule applies.
- **Filing method:** MyFTB for Withholding Agents (payment online or with Form 592-V), SWIFT (required over 250 payees; mail only Form 592-V with a payment), or paper to Withholding Services and Compliance MS F182, Franchise Tax Board, PO Box 942867, Sacramento CA 94267-0651 (2026 Form 592 instructions).
- **Amending:** file an amended Form 592 for the same taxable year; no negative numbers; attach a letter explaining the corrections. A form filed for the wrong year needs a new correct-year form and a zeroed amended form for the wrong year (2026 Form 592 instructions).
- **Records:** at least five years (2026 Form 592 instructions; Pub. 1017 Q49).

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Each payee classified as resident, exempt (valid Form 590 or California street address), or nonresident; waivers and reductions on file.
- [ ] Each payment classified by type and California source; Form 587 or another reasonable allocation used for mixed work.
- [ ] Year-to-date payments to each payee tracked against USD 1,500.
- [ ] Backup withholding cases identified and withheld at 7% instead of other withholding.
- [ ] 7% computed on the California source amount; property management fees and approved reductions applied.
- [ ] Form 592 and payment filed by each period's due date, with all payees listed.
- [ ] Forms 592-B furnished to payees by 31 January.
